Element of Ink
Private proposal · Amanda

Website / Digital flagship

A digital home worthy of the shop.

The goal was never to make Element of Ink look like another tattoo website. It was to make the digital experience carry the same confidence, atmosphere and attention to detail people expect when they walk into a premium studio.

The site should feel expensive before anyone sees a price. It now works less like a brochure and more like a piece of the brand: cinematic, tactile, slightly unsettling, and unmistakably built around Amanda's work.

01 / What is already built

The complete experience.

This is not a template with photos dropped into it. The current build includes custom design, custom motion, custom image-processing logic and responsive behavior built specifically around Element of Ink.

Art direction

Premium editorial layout

Cinematic composition, asymmetric spacing, restrained gold detailing, custom typography, deliberate negative space and a portfolio hierarchy designed to make the work feel collectible rather than tiled.

Hero experience

Cinematic first impression

Full-screen tattoo video treatment, studio messaging, specialty positioning, booking access and graceful still-image behavior for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

Artist story

Dedicated Amanda feature

A standalone editorial artist section using Amanda's throne portrait, specialties and positioning instead of burying the artist inside the gallery.

Portfolio

Art-directed selected work

Responsive gallery composition with deliberately varied image scale, captions, indexing, custom crops and thirteen production tattoo masks built from Amanda's actual photographs.

Signature interaction

Ink that physically moves

A custom tattoo interaction where the image stays still while detected tattoo structures lift, repel from the tattoo-machine cursor, reveal sampled bare skin beneath them, and spring precisely back into place.

Image engineering

Organic segmentation

Ink-derived connected components, irregular fragment growth, skin-tone replacement, fine-line and shading detection, depth, shadow and local interaction instead of generic square-pixel effects.

Custom cursor

Tattoo machine pointer

A purpose-built tattoo-machine cursor with the interaction hotspot aligned near the needle tip and context-sensitive visual behavior over interactive work.

Atmosphere

Smoke, ink and motion

Major headings materialize through drifting smoke and ink blooms while supporting copy resolves more quietly as the visitor scrolls.

Narrative detail

Subtle skeleton cameo

An articulated skeletal figure quietly forms in smoke, stands, takes a few restrained steps and disappears again—designed as a blink-and-you-missed-it studio detail rather than a gimmick.

Interactive feature

Practice skin

An on-site drawing experience lets visitors activate the needle, draw on simulated skin and watch the line hold before fading.

Sound / ambience

Optional studio audio

A visitor-controlled sound system supports the atmosphere without forcing audio playback, with multiple tracks already wired into the experience.

Conversion

Booking pathway

Persistent access to booking, a premium final inquiry section, direct Instagram and email links, and a simple booking request flow that gets the first conversation started.

Responsive

Desktop to phone

The composition changes intentionally across desktop, tablet and mobile rather than merely shrinking. Heavy pointer effects are limited where they do not make sense on touch devices.

Accessibility

Motion with an off switch

Reduced-motion support, keyboard focus states, semantic sections and sensible fallbacks keep the theatrical parts from becoming a usability problem.

Discoverability

Search-ready foundation

Page metadata and structured TattooParlor business markup give search engines clearer information about Element of Ink. A deeper local SEO program can be added later.

Performance

Built to stay lean

Lazy-loaded portfolio imagery, modular JavaScript and effects that initialize only when appropriate keep the site from becoming a giant animation demo disguised as a business website.

Amanda's throne portrait is intentionally excluded from the tattoo-lift interaction. The site treats the artist as the artist, not as another interactive tattoo image.

Friend price. Real value.

The point of a friend rate is to make the project easier to say yes to—not to pretend the work is worth less than it is.

03 / Easy edits

Things we can change.

The existing design is a strong direction, not a prison. These are normal refinement items we can tune around Amanda and the actual shop before launch.

  • Rewrite any headline, tagline, bio or booking language.
  • Change the hero video or hero still image.
  • Reorder, remove or add portfolio pieces.
  • Adjust individual tattoo crops and focal points.
  • Increase or reduce the tattoo-lift intensity.
  • Change how close the cursor must get before ink reacts.
  • Make smoke reveals heavier, lighter, faster or slower.
  • Make the skeleton easier or harder to notice—or remove it entirely.
  • Change colours, gold warmth, background depth or typography scale.
  • Replace or remove music and ambient audio.
  • Change booking questions and inquiry wording.
  • Add the shop address, hours, phone number and walk-in policy.
  • Change Instagram, email, domain and contact destinations.
  • Add artist credentials, specialties or a fuller personal story.
  • Change the practice-skin experience or remove it.
  • Tune mobile spacing, crops and section order independently from desktop.

05 / Boundaries

No surprise bullshit.

The proposal should be easy to understand. The website itself is one project. Anything that turns it into a booking platform, store or ongoing marketing operation is separate and only happens if Amanda wants it.

Included

Current design and interactions, reasonable final refinements, production deployment, chosen-domain connection and launch handoff.

Not forced

No required maintenance plan, advertising package, SEO retainer, booking software subscription or long contract.

Third parties

Domain fees, hosting upgrades, Stripe fees, scheduling platforms or other paid services are passed through only when selected.

06 / The point

Make the first impression match the chair.

The work already has the quality. The shop already has the atmosphere. The website's job is to make a stranger feel some of that before they ever walk through the door—and give the right client a clean next step when they decide they want Amanda's work on them.
